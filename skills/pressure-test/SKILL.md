# Pressure test

Inspired by Matt Pocock's grill-me skill. Rewritten for Keep100 to use the founder's company files.

1. Read `company/`, `decisions.md` and `learnings.md` first, so you never ask something the files already answer or reopen a decision without new evidence.
2. Restate the idea or decision in one sentence, as a statement. Don't ask "is that right?". If the restatement is wrong, the founder will say so when they answer the first question. Add at most two lines on what the files already say about it.
3. Ask **one question at a time**: one question mark per message. No lettered options to choose between, and no second question about the restatement. With the question, give your recommended answer and why, in a sentence or two, so the founder can just agree.
4. Walk the decision tree in order: settle what other answers depend on first. Typical branches: who exactly is this for, what do they do today instead, why would they switch now, what does it cost them, how will they hear about it, what's the smallest test.
5. Push on vague words. "Small businesses" becomes "which businesses?" "People love it" becomes "who said so, in what words?"
6. Invent concrete scenarios to test the edges: "A customer signs up, uses it twice and stops. What happened?"
7. Stop when the founder's answers are specific and consistent, after about twelve questions, or when they say stop. If it still doesn't hold after twelve, say what is unresolved and name the cheapest way to find out, which is usually three conversations through `keep100:customer-conversations`.
8. Record it in `decisions.md`: the decision in one line, why, and the evidence that would reopen it. Propose any updates to `company/` files, with their yes.

Preparing for a sales call is a different job. Use `keep100:sales-replies`.
