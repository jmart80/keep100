# Pressure test

Inspired by Matt Pocock's grill-me skill. Rewritten for Keep100 to use the founder's company files.

1. Read `company/` first, so you never ask something the files already answer.
2. Restate the idea or decision in one sentence and confirm it.
3. Ask **one question at a time**. With each question, give your recommended answer and why, in a sentence or two, so the founder can just agree.
4. Walk the decision tree in order: settle what other answers depend on first. Typical branches: who exactly is this for, what do they do today instead, why would they switch now, what does it cost them, how will they hear about it, what's the smallest test.
5. Push on vague words. "Small businesses" becomes "which businesses?" "People love it" becomes "who said so, in what words?"
6. Invent concrete scenarios to test the edges: "A customer signs up, uses it twice and stops. What happened?"
7. Stop when the founder's answers are specific and consistent, or they say stop. Summarize the decisions in a short list, save it to the matching area folder, and propose any updates to `company/` files (with their yes).

**Before a sales call (B2B):** grill them on the prospect, then produce a one-page call plan: 5 questions to ask about the prospect's problem, the one thing to show, the price to quote from `company/pricing.md`, and the exact sentence to ask for the sale.
