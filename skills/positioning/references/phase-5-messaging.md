# Phase 5 Reference: Messaging and Homepage

Kramer sets the guide and the page skeleton, Fletch sets the hero, Copyhackers fills the copy, Laja grades it.

## The one-page messaging guide (Kramer / MKT1)

Most messaging guides are nine pages too long. One page, three components:

1. **USP:** `[Product] is a [what/category] for [who/ideal customer] that delivers [outcome] through [differentiators]`
2. **Three value props:** the three most desirable, differentiated benefits; three legs of a stool supporting the USP promise. Pull directly from Phase 4.
3. **Proof points per value prop:** (a) features or functionality, (b) social proof or testimonials, (c) quantified results.

## The Fletch homepage process

1. **Audit current messaging** with the 8-component model (persona, company type, context, problem; category, capability, feature, benefit). Mark each element present, absent, or broken. Skip when there is no existing page.
2. **Identify the minimum viable positioning:** the one combination of segment, use case, capability, and differentiation that most clearly communicates what the product is. This becomes the hero.
3. **Pick the 2 to 3 most differentiated value props** that speak to the champion's pains. Everything cannot be messaged.
4. **Translate to page structure.**

**Hero formula:** H1 names the specific use case or problem being solved, as specific as possible. H2 lists the 2 to 3 product capabilities that make it possible, explicitly. The hero encodes the entire positioning in two lines.

## MKT1 homepage section order

| Section | Content | Messaging element |
|---|---|---|
| Top nav | Links, sign-in, primary CTA | |
| Hero | Top messaging blocks plus product screenshot or video | USP: problem, solution, who it is for, why it is better |
| Social proof | Logos, optional quotes. Write the heading specifically: "Join 3,000 engineering managers," never "Companies love us" | Reinforces ICP specificity |
| 2 to 3 benefit sections | Product screenshots (preferred over illustrations, they double as proof), each with proof points | Value props plus proof |
| Use case or persona section | 3 use cases or 3 segments, linking to dedicated pages | Secondary persona coverage |
| How it works (optional) | Diagram. Required for complex products or new categories | For products without an existing category assumption |
| CTA bar | Restate the message, add urgency, primary CTA. Always above the footer | Conversion close |

Rules: minimal hero copy, one primary CTA, screenshots over abstractions.

## Copy formulas (Wiebe / Copyhackers)

Every formula gets filled with verbatims from the corpus, never invented phrasing. Write many candidates (Wiebe's calibration: 50 headlines before one goes on the page), then cut hard. Clear always beats clever.

| Formula | Template | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity/proof | `[Numeral] + [noun] + [verb] + [object]` | Established products with measurable outcomes |
| Do hard thing fast | `[Do something hard] in [period of time]` | Speed or efficiency differentiation |
| Outcome by method | `[Desired outcome] by [how]` | When the mechanism is the differentiator |
| Helping X do Y | `Helping [ICP] do [desired outcome]` | Early stage; forces audience plus outcome specificity |
| Like X, unlike anything | `Like a [known thing]. Unlike any [known category].` | Existing category with a clear distinction |

**PAS for below-the-fold sections:** Problem (name it in VOC language), Agitate (make the reader feel it), Solve (introduce the solution once they are primed). Use when pain is high and the reader is in the struggling moment.

**Wiebe's messaging hierarchy.** The page answers the prospect's questions in this order:

1. What do you do?
2. Why should I care?
3. Am I alone in caring, or do others like me care?
4. I am starting to be won over, but I am skeptical.
5. What is in it for me specifically?

## Grading and validation (Laja / Wynter)

**The five layers, addressed strictly in order.** A message that is relevant but unclear does not convert; differentiated but irrelevant does not convert either.

| Layer | Question | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Do they get it? | Jargon, category confusion, vague claims |
| Relevance | Is it about what they care about right now? | Talking features instead of their priorities |
| Value | Do they want the promised outcome? | Capabilities instead of outcomes |
| Differentiation | Why choose this over alternatives? | Looking identical to every competitor |
| Friction | What doubts does it create? | Unsubstantiated or anxiety-inducing claims |

Wynter's buyer journey data: B2B buyers shortlist through peer recommendations, brand fame, third-party reviews, and search, without talking to sales, then narrow to about 3 vendors by asking how each is different and better for their use cases. The website is the primary weapon in shortlisting; differentiated value must be clear before a demo is ever requested. In B2B nobody cares about the why before the what is crystal clear.

**Validation options, by budget:**

1. **Wynter message test:** 10 to 20 ICP-qualified panelists score each section on clarity, relevance, value, and differentiation, plus open-ended reactions. Fix the lowest-scoring layer first, then re-test.
2. **Five-second test:** show the hero for five seconds to 20 to 50 participants; ask what the product does, who it is for, what stood out. Tests clarity and first impression only.
3. **Sales call feedback loop:** track whether reps use the approved language and whether prospects express confusion. Dunford's most reliable positioning signal, and free.
4. **Peer review:** 5 to 10 ICP-matched peers (not colleagues) answering the Wynter questions manually.
