# Phase 4: The message

Everything here is translation, not invention. The inputs exist: struggles from Phase 1, what only we have from Phase 2, the champion from Phase 3.

## Pain, capability, outcome

| Research input | Message layer | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| The struggle, in their words | Pain, the before state | Headline, problem section |
| What the alternatives can't do | Capability | Subheadline, value props |
| What they wanted | Outcome, the after state | Value props, final button |

**Build it:** for each top struggle, pair the capability that resolves it and the outcome the buyer described. Drop any pain the product does not resolve better than the alternatives. That pain belongs to the category.

**Value prop form:** one pain, one capability, one outcome, in the buyer's vocabulary. Write three. Each traces to at least one quote and one thing only we have. A value prop with no quote behind it is invented copy and gets cut.

## The job and the objection

- **Job to be done:** the task the buyer is hiring the product for, as a verb phrase in their words. "Track client visits and file session notes", not "manage their work".
- **Top objection:** the main reason a buyer who wants it still hesitates. Take it from the hesitation quotes. With none, say so and default to trust in a new product.

## Finding the words (Matt Lerner, First Round Review)

The words are right when a prospect reads them and feels understood. Finding them comes before any clever copywriting.

1. Go back to the quotes: what was in the buyer's head before they found a solution?
2. Draft headlines in that language. Never internal names or feature labels.
3. Test for understanding, free: show the headline and subheadline to five people who fit the ideal customer for five seconds each. Ask what the product does and who it's for. Keep the versions people can play back.

Write up to ten candidates, each with the quote it came from, ranked by how often and how strongly that struggle appeared.

## Headline formulas (Joanna Wiebe, Copyhackers)

Fill each with words from the quotes. Write many, cut hard. Clear beats clever.

| Formula | Template | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Do a hard thing fast | {Do something hard} in {time} | Speed is the difference, and the time is a confirmed fact |
| Outcome by method | {Outcome} by {how} | The mechanism is the difference |
| Helping X do Y | Helping {who} {outcome} | Early stage. Forces a clear audience |
| Like X, unlike any | Like {known thing}. Unlike any {category}. | A known category with one clear difference |

A formula that needs a number may only use a confirmed one.

## The hero in two lines (Fletch)

The headline names the specific use case or problem. The subheadline names the two or three capabilities that make it possible. Together they carry the whole position.

## Grading the message (Peep Laja, Wynter)

Check five things in order. Fix the first one that fails before looking at the next.

| Layer | Question | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Do they get it? | Jargon, a vague category |
| Relevance | Is it about what they care about now? | Features instead of their priorities |
| Value | Do they want the outcome? | Capabilities with no result |
| Difference | Why this over the alternatives? | Sounds like every competitor |
| Friction | What doubt does it create? | Claims with nothing behind them |

Free ways to check: the five-second test above, asking the next five people in conversations to play the message back, and watching which lines prospects repeat in their replies.
