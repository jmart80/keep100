# Phase 3: Who to win first

Voje picks the segment, Rachitsky sharpens it, Kramer turns it into a person.

## The beachhead (Maja Voje, Go-To-Market Strategist)

The customer a founder wants at scale usually needs case studies, references and credibility that don't exist yet. The beachhead is the group that can be won now. Winning it builds the proof that opens the bigger market.

**Score each candidate segment, high, medium or low:**

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Burning pain | The problem exists today and they are already spending time or money on it |
| Willingness to pay | They pay for something similar now |
| Reachability | The founder can find and contact them this month |
| Testimonial potential | They would let their name be used |
| Bridge | Winning them leads somewhere bigger |

**Process:**

1. List every group that has the problem.
2. Score each. Use quotes and conversations as the evidence, and say when a score is a guess.
3. Pick the highest.
4. Check that they are looking for a solution and not only being polite. Liking the idea is not paying for it. A pre-sale, a paid pilot or a waitlist with a real question tests this. `keep100:offer` sets one up.

When two segments score close, pick the one the founder can reach faster, and note the other under what to learn.

## Three sharp criteria (Lenny Rachitsky)

Pick the three most defining traits from this list and make each specific:

1. Size of company, or life stage of the person
2. Job title or role
3. The pain being solved
4. Their particular way of working
5. Tools they already use
6. Type of business
7. Where they are
8. A specific place they spend time, such as a named community

Start from who gets most excited about what is being built.

## The champion (Emily Kramer, MKT1)

Write for one person: the one most likely to start the purchase. A page that speaks to several kinds of buyer at once speaks to none of them clearly. Messages for anyone else involved come later.

Describe the champion in five lines, each from the Phase 1 quotes:

1. **Pains:** the problems they need solved
2. **Gains:** the outcomes they want
3. **Triggers:** the events that make them look now
4. **Blockers:** what holds them back
5. **Motivators:** what gives them the confidence to buy

For B2B, add one line on who else has to agree. For B2C, describe the person and the moment they start looking, and skip the rest.

Triggers come from Bourgoin's question. Blockers come from Moesta's anxiety and habit. If Phase 1 was done well, this assembles itself.
