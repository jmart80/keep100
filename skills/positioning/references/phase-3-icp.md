# Phase 3 Reference: ICP Definition

Voje picks the segment, Rachitsky sharpens it, Kramer turns it into people. Keep the three layers distinct.

## The three layers (Kramer / MKT1)

| Layer | Definition | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **ICP** | Company-level profile of the ideal account | Firmographics: size, industry, stage, tech stack, growth rate, business model |
| **Persona** | Individual role inside the ICP company | Pains, gains, shifts, blockers, motivators |
| **Buying committee** | All roles in the purchase decision | Champion (initiates), economic buyer (approves budget), evaluators, end users, influencers |

**Kramer's rule:** write the homepage for the champion, the person most likely to initiate the buying process. Multi-persona homepages consistently underperform single-persona homepages at early stage. Persona-specific messaging for other committee members comes later, on dedicated pages.

## Beachhead scoring (Maja Voje, Go-To-Market Strategist)

**ECP versus ICP.** The ICP is the company you want to serve at scale; it usually requires case studies, references, and compliance credibility you do not have yet. The ECP (early customer profile) is the beachhead you can actually win in the next 3 to 18 months. Start with the ECP to build the proof that unlocks the ICP. Chasing the ICP too early traps a startup in long sales cycles with nothing to close.

**Scoring criteria.** An ideal beachhead checks all of:

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Burning pain | "Shut up and take my money" urgency; the problem exists today |
| Willingness to pay | Proven ability and willingness to pay for a solution |
| Reachability | You can reach them and close within 3 to 18 months |
| Testimonial potential | They will give case studies and peer recommendations |
| Bridge to bigger market | The segment is a stepping stone, not a dead end |

**Process:**

1. Map all segments experiencing the problem.
2. Score each segment on the criteria (1 to 3 scale or high/med/low).
3. Select the highest combined score.
4. Validate that the winner is actively looking for a solution, not just complimentary. Liking the idea is not paying for it. Validation methods: paid tests measuring real intent, waitlists, presales, co-creation with a pilot client. When two or three segments score close, run parallel tests and watch for early retention, expansion, and monetization signals before committing.

Voje's market-problem map plots segments on urgency of problem against reachability; the high-urgency high-reachability quadrant is the beachhead zone.

## Rachitsky's ICP checklist (Lenny's Newsletter)

Pick the 3 or so most defining criteria from this list and get super-specific on each. Start by paying attention to who gets most excited about what is being built.

1. Company size
2. Job title
3. Pain point being solved
4. The company's unique way of working (design-driven, operationally heavy)
5. Specific tech used (data warehouse, GitLab)
6. Type of business (B2B SaaS, e-commerce)
7. Price point of the customer's own product
8. Geo
9. A unique place the user spends time (a specific community)

Gupta's complement: start from what they are craving and what frustrates them daily, and build a competitor feature database to find counter-positioning room. A launch aimed at everyone misses everyone.

## Kramer's 5-factor champion persona (via Allyson Letteri, MKT1)

Every persona in the packet contains all five:

1. **Pains:** the problems the persona needs solved
2. **Gains:** the outcomes they are seeking
3. **Shifts:** the trigger events that make them consider a new solution (feed these from the Phase 1 trigger data)
4. **Blockers:** objections and obstacles holding them back
5. **Motivators:** what gives them the confidence to buy

Shifts come straight from Bourgoin's trigger question. Blockers come straight from Moesta's anxiety and habit forces. If Phase 1 was done properly, this persona assembles itself from the corpus.
