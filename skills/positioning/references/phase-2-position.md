# Phase 2: The position

Dunford supplies the logic. Fletch turns it into something a homepage can say.

## Dunford's five components (Obviously Awesome)

Positioning defines how the product is the best at something a defined set of customers cares a lot about. It is not a tagline. Work the components in order, because each is the input to the next.

| # | Component | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Competitive alternatives | What would they do if we didn't exist? |
| 2 | Unique attributes | What do we have that the alternatives don't? |
| 3 | Value | So what, for the customer? |
| 4 | Who cares most | Who gets the most from that value? |
| 5 | Market category | What would they call us? |

**How to work it, as one person:**

1. **Start from the people who fit best.** With customers, the ones who bought fast and love it. With none, the people in conversations and reviews with the strongest pain. With neither, say so, stay loose, and list what to learn.
2. **Let go of the default.** The founder's first description of the product usually comes from how it was built. Set it aside before starting.
3. **List the real alternatives.** Direct competitors, bent-to-fit tools such as spreadsheets, a person they pay, and doing nothing. Expect two to five groups. A difference only exists relative to these.
4. **Isolate what only we have.** Stay at the level of capabilities here. Benefits come next. Use `product.md` and only what is live.
5. **Turn each attribute into value.** Ask "so what, for the customer?" and group the answers into one to three themes. Each theme needs proof from `facts.md`, or is marked unproven.
6. **Find who cares most.** Role or kind of person, situation, the trigger. Narrow is correct.
7. **Choose the category.** Three options: compete head-on in a known category, own a small corner of a known category, or name a new one. A new founder nearly always wants the second. A new category has to be explained before it can be sold, which costs time a solo founder doesn't have.

**Mistakes to check for:** letting customers pick the category by default, describing the roadmap instead of what ships today, forgetting that doing nothing is a competitor, and reaching the difference too late in the pitch.

## Fletch's anchor decision (Pierri and Kaminski, Fletch PMM)

**Core idea:** positioning is product differentiation plus customer segmentation.

Decide the anchor, and record it:

- **Category-based:** anchor in a category people already understand, plus what's different. Works when buyers search for the category by name.
- **Use-case-based:** anchor in a specific, familiar task. Works when the category is crowded or unclear and the task is obvious.

Choose whichever makes the value easier to explain to this buyer.

**Lead with what the product does.** Fletch's advice runs against the usual "lead with benefits": open with the concrete capability and end with the outcome, because the capability is what is different. Lead with the audience's problem instead when the product is broad or the capability needs explaining.

## Category awareness

Record one of three in `positioning.md`. The landing page skill picks its headline type from it.

- **Known:** buyers already search for this category by name.
- **Emerging:** some do, and most describe the problem instead.
- **Unknown:** nobody searches for it yet.
