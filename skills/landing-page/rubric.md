# Quality Rubric

20 binary checks. Score the full draft; a failing check sends the affected section back to drafting. Report every check with pass/fail and one line of evidence. Word limits live beside each template in `templates.md`. The banned list is Keep100's one list, held and checked by `keep100:writing-check`.

## Clarity (the stranger test)

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category present in hero | Headline + subheadline contain a word or phrase a buyer would use to search for this product category |
| 2 | Ideal customer named in hero | Hero names who it is for so a visitor can tell if that's them: a role or company type (B2B), a person in a situation (B2C), or the job to be done. Not bare "you", "teams", "businesses", "everyone" |
| 3 | Competitive alternative named | A specific tool name, category description, or vivid process appears in the hero or Problem section; not "legacy tools" or "traditional approaches" |
| 4 | Hero passes the stranger test | From headline + subheadline alone, a stranger can state what the product is, who it is for, and what it does differently |
| 5 | Hero is clean of banned vocabulary | The writing-check script reports no banned term in the headline or subheadline |

## Concreteness

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Outcomes are falsifiable | Every outcome includes a unit (time, money, %, count) or a named before/after state; no bare "faster", "better", "save time" |
| 7 | Capabilities, not abstractions | Every value prop block describes what the user DOES, with an action verb; no bare feature names or feeling statements |
| 8 | Claims are falsifiable | Every claim could be investigated and disproven (specific numbers, timeframes, certifications); nothing structurally unfalsifiable ("proven approach", "significant results") |

## Anchoring

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Outcome anchored to mechanism | Every stated outcome is paired, in the same section, with the capability or feature that produces it |
| 10 | Customer language | No banned term page-wide, and customer phrasing from `company/customers.md` used wherever a `[verbatim]` quote fits. Nothing is shown in quotation marks unless it is tagged `[verbatim]` |
| 11 | Job-to-outcome connection | The hero's primary outcome connects directly to "Job to be done" in `company/positioning.md`. Not a distant business metric when the job is a functional task |

## Structure

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Word limits respected | Every word limit stated beside its template in `templates.md` is met |
| 13 | Problem names the alternative | Section 2 uses `{competitive_alternative}` from "Alternatives we beat" in `company/positioning.md`, in one of the three approved formulas |
| 14 | Every pain gets paid off | Each pain raised in section 2 is answered by section 3 or a section 4 block |
| 15 | FAQ is objection-driven | 5-6 questions, all from the objection categories for the founder's track in `templates.md`, each answered in 1-3 sentences from confirmed facts, `{objection}` first |
| 16 | No new claims in Final CTA | The Final CTA section introduces zero value props not already on the page |

## CTA strength

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | Single primary CTA | One dominant primary CTA above the fold, repeated with identical text in the Final CTA section, and pointing at the link in `company/product.md` |
| 18 | CTA copy is specific | Primary CTA names an action or value in 2-5 words; not "Get started", "Submit", "Learn more" |
| 19 | Microcopy addresses the top objection | Primary CTA has microcopy of 8 words or fewer that addresses the top objection and does not repeat the button text |
| 20 | Final CTA echoes the hero outcome | Final CTA headline restates the hero's primary outcome in 8 words or fewer |
