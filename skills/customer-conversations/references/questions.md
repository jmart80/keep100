# Question bank

Pick five or six. Every question asks about something that already happened. Follow each answer with "what happened next?" or "what did that cost you?" until the answer contains a date, a number or a name.

## Opener

"I'm trying to understand how people handle {problem area}. I'm not selling anything today. Can you walk me through the last time {the situation} came up?"

## The story of the problem

| To learn | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Whether the problem is real for them | "When did this last happen? Talk me through that day." | "Is this a problem for you?" |
| How much it hurts | "What did it cost you, in hours or money or something else?" | "How painful is it, one to ten?" |
| What triggers a search | "What was going on when you first decided the old way wasn't working?" | "What would make you look for a tool?" |
| What they tried | "What have you tried so far? What happened with each?" | "Have you looked at alternatives?" |
| What they use now | "Show me, or describe, exactly how you do it today." | "What tools do you use?" |
| What they pay | "What are you paying for that, including your own time?" | "What would you pay for a solution?" |
| Who else is involved | "Who else touches this? Who would have to agree to change it?" | "Are you the decision maker?" |
| What stops a switch | "The last time you changed a tool like this, what almost stopped you?" | "Any concerns?" |

## If they already use the product

- "What were you doing before? What made you look for something else?"
- "What nearly stopped you from signing up?"
- "What's the last thing you used it for?"
- "If it disappeared tomorrow, what would you do instead?"
- "What did you expect it to do that it doesn't?"

## Words to listen for

Write these down exactly: the name they give the problem, the name they give the category of product, anything said with feeling, any comparison ("it's like...").

## Closing

- "Is there anything I should have asked?"
- "Who else deals with this that I should talk to?"
- When the problem is clearly theirs: "I'm building something for this. Would you {see it for 15 minutes next week | try it with your next {job} | join the first ten customers at {the founding offer in company/pricing.md}}?"

A no at this point is useful. Ask what would have made it a yes.

## Reading the answers

| They say | It means |
|---|---|
| "That sounds great" | Nothing. Log as a compliment |
| "I would definitely use that" | Nothing. A prediction about the future |
| "We tried X and Y and built a spreadsheet" | They have the problem and have spent effort on it |
| "Can I get it this week?" | Ask for the commitment now |
| "Send me something" | A polite no, unless they name what and by when |
| A feature request | Ask what they would do with it and what they do without it today |
